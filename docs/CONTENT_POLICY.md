# Content & Safety Policy

**Status:** DRAFT. The owner must approve it. Have a Tennessee media attorney review it before taking paid sponsors.
**Applies to:** every video, caption, comment reply and link page we publish.

This policy exists for three reasons:

- to avoid harming people who are having the worst day of their lives
- to avoid publishing anything false
- to keep the channel eligible for platform monetization and safe for sponsors

Check every video against these rules before you post it.

---

## 1. What we publish

- Incidents that appear on the Hamilton County 9-1-1 public Active Incidents list, described **only** with facts shown there: type, event, status, agency, time, block-level location and area.
- Road closures and traffic-impacting incidents.
- Aggregates and trends, such as "burglaries reported in East Ridge this week."
- Official agency statements, quoted and linked, when available.

## 2. Code Red calls

Every call on the county list is posted. Most get full details. A call becomes **CODE RED** only when its topic would cause trouble on TikTok or YouTube, or would expose a private person's health or abuse situation.

A Code Red shows a red map pin, the street block, the area, the dispatch time and one neutral label. **Nothing else.** No event, agency, status, incident number or commentary, on screen, in the voiceover or in the caption. The scraper removes those fields before saving.

| Label | Calls | Platform reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Medical Response** | All EMS calls; medical conditions, overdoses, falls, sick persons; suicide, self-harm and mental-health calls | YouTube treats drugs, suicide and self-harm as sensitive topics. TikTok can pull behavioral-health content from the For You feed. These are also private health matters. |
| **Police Response** | Domestic and family violence, protective orders; sexual offenses and indecent exposure; child-related calls (juvenile, child, CPS, runaway, missing persons); death investigations; welfare checks | YouTube limits ads when child abuse is the main topic. Both platforms treat sexual and domestic abuse as sensitive. These calls can identify victims. |

**Posted with full details:** burglary, theft, shoplifting, stolen vehicles, vandalism, robbery, shots fired and shootings, stabbings, assaults, fights, homicides, kidnappings, weapons calls, fires, alarms, wrecks (including injury wrecks), road closures, disorders and suspicious persons. Both platforms allow non-graphic news reporting of crime; YouTube lists "reports of nearby homicide events without graphic descriptions" as eligible for full ads.

What keeps violent-crime posts safe:

- **Facts from the 911 list only.** No descriptions of injuries, blood or weapons beyond the event name.
- **No graphic footage or images.** Map and text only.
- **"Reported" or "dispatched" wording.** TikTok can limit reach on unverified information about an unfolding emergency, so every post says where the information comes from.

Don't guess or hint at what a Code Red was, even in comments. If a viewer asks, reply: "We only share the location for these calls."

## 3. What we never publish

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **No names, faces, license plates or house numbers** of victims, suspects or bystanders | Privacy and defamation risk; TikTok treats private home addresses as personal information |
| **No exact addresses.** Show the street block only ("1200 block of Boynton Dr"). Map pins snap to about 100 m. | Locations of homes |
| **No speculation** about cause, motive, suspects, injuries or outcome | Defamation and accuracy |
| **No graphic descriptions** of injuries, deaths or violence | YouTube removes ads from graphic accounts of shootings and tragedies |
| **No incidents at schools, shelters, hospitals or places of worship** unless an agency has issued a public statement | Sensitive locations |
| **No Breaking Clips on calls still marked `R` (Reported).** Wait until units are Enroute or On Scene. | Many reported calls turn out to be nothing |
| **No scanner audio** until a legal review is done | Federal and state law limits; Broadcastify terms |

## 4. Language rules

- Always use "**reported**", "**dispatched**" or "**responded to**". Never write "a burglary happened" or "a shooting occurred."
- Use the event wording from the county list. Don't escalate it: "Shots Fired Call" never becomes "shooting."
- No fear-bait hooks. "Is your street safe?" is fine; "Chattanooga is a war zone" is not.
- Times are stated as "around 2 p.m.", with the dispatch time, not the time of the crime.
- Every video carries the on-screen line: **"Based on public 9-1-1 dispatch data. Not an official statement. Details may change."**
- Use your own voice. If you ever use an AI voice or AI visuals, turn on the platform's AI-generated content label.

## 5. Accuracy & corrections

- **Every fact must come from the 911 list or an official source.** If you can't point to where it came from, cut it.
- **Corrections SLA:** a reported error gets a pinned correction comment within **2 hours** during waking hours. If the error is material, the video is deleted and re-posted corrected within **24 hours**.
- A pinned "How this channel works / corrections" video, plus a link-page form, gives people a way to report errors.
- Log every correction in a simple notes file or sheet: the date, video, what was wrong and the action taken.

## 6. Takedown requests

People named or shown (by mistake), property owners, and agencies can request removal through the link-page form or by email.

- **Agency requests** are honored immediately. No argument.
- **Individual requests** about their own home or identity: remove within **24 hours**.
- Log every request, as in §5.

## 7. Comments

- Hide or delete comments that name suspects or victims, make threats, or give exact addresses.
- Use keyword filters on both platforms: slurs, "address is", "lives at", phone-number patterns.
- Don't argue with comments. Pin the facts.

## 8. Sponsors

- Sponsor segments are visually separate and labeled "**Sponsored**." Turn on the platform's paid-partnership toggle every time.
- **Sponsors never appear inside a Breaking Clip about one incident.** They appear only in recaps, Weekly Top 5 and explainers, so no business is shown next to a specific victim's incident.
- We don't accept sponsors whose pitch exploits fear, such as "buy a gun before it's too late." Security, insurance, locksmith and similar offers are fine when framed around prevention.
- Affiliate links carry an FTC disclosure on the link page and in the caption ("#ad / affiliate links").

## 9. Using the 911 list

- Read the list and write the facts in your own words. Don't screen-record or reuse the county's page or map, which is marked "All Rights Reserved"; use your own Google Maps screenshots instead.
- Credit the source in captions: "Source: Hamilton County 9-1-1 public incident list."
- If the 9-1-1 district asks you to change how you use their information, do what they ask.
