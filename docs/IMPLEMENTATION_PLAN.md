# Chattanooga Crime & Safety: TikTok + YouTube Shorts Page Plan

**Start:** Tue Oct 13, 2026 · **Plan length:** 90 days, to Jan 10, 2027
**What it is:** a short-form local news page. Every day you check the public Hamilton County 9-1-1 Active Incidents list, pick the notable calls, and post short videos with your voice over a simple map graphic. Nothing to code.
**Goal:** money. Local sponsors and affiliate links come first, then platform payouts as the page grows.

**Tools (all free or cheap):** your phone, CapCut (editing and auto-captions), Canva (cover templates), Google Maps (screenshots or screen recordings), a link-in-bio page (Beacons or Linktree), and a Gmail.
**Source:** https://www.hamiltontn911.gov/active-incidents.php. You can also cross-check against local news (WRCB, WTVC, Chattanooga Times Free Press) and the police and sheriff Facebook pages.

| **YouTube full Partner Program** (ad revenue) | 1,000 subscribers and 10M Shorts views in 90 days | Month 3+ |

**Two rules that protect payouts:**

1. **Use your own voice and your own edit on every video.** YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy rejects channels built from templated AI output, slideshows, or AI narration over stock footage. Your voice and commentary are what make the page monetizable.
2. **Make the daily recap over 60 seconds.** Only videos over one minute count toward TikTok Creator Rewards.

## 2. Content formats

| Format | Length | When | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Daily Recap** (main show) | 61–90 s | Every evening, 6–8 p.m. | Hook ("5 calls you should know about in Chattanooga today") → 4–6 incidents with map pins → takeaway / safety tip → "follow for tomorrow's" |
| **Breaking Clip** | 15–30 s | When something big happens: a major fire, road closure, large police response | "Heavy police response reported on ___ right now" + map + "avoid the area" |
| **Weekly Top 5** | 60–90 s | Sundays | Which neighborhoods had the most calls this week. Good for sponsors. |
| **Explainer** | 30–60 s | 1–2 a week | "What 'Enroute' and 'On Scene' mean," "Why the 911 list doesn't show every call," "How to read the 911 map" |
| **Road & Traffic** | 15–30 s | Weekday mornings, 7–8 a.m. | Closures and wrecks on I-24, I-75, Hwy 153 and Hixson Pike |

**Make one video, post it twice:** export once from CapCut, then upload it to TikTok and as a YouTube Short. Don't repost a TikTok with its watermark to YouTube.

## 3. Daily routine (about 60–90 minutes)

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 7:00 a.m. (10 min) | Check the 911 list for overnight calls and road closures. Post a Road & Traffic clip if there's one worth posting. |
| During the day (5 min, 2–3 times) | Check the list and screenshot notable calls. Post a Breaking Clip if something big is happening. |
| 5:30 p.m. (45 min) | Pick 4–6 calls → take Google Maps screenshots → record the voiceover → edit in CapCut (captions, map pins, disclaimer line) → export |
| 7:00 p.m. (10 min) | Post the Daily Recap to TikTok and YouTube Shorts, with the caption, hashtags and location tag |
| 7:00–7:30 p.m. | Reply to comments, pin the facts, hide comments that name people |
| (15 min) | Log the day's numbers in the tracker. From week 3: 3 sponsor follow-ups. |

## 8. Rules that keep the page safe and paid

Full detail is in `docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md`. The short version:

- Say "**reported**" and "**police responded to**," never "a shooting happened." Facts only from the 911 list or official sources.
- **Never post** medical or EMS calls, domestic or child-related calls, or suicide or mental-health calls.
- **Street block only** ("1200 block of Boynton Dr"). No names, faces, plates or house numbers.
- On-screen line in every video: "**Based on public 9-1-1 dispatch data. Not an official statement.**"
- Use your own map screenshots (Google Maps with its logo visible), not screen recordings of the county's page, which is marked "All Rights Reserved."
- Fix errors fast: pin a correction within 2 hours, and delete and repost if it's serious.
- Sponsors go in recaps only, never next to a single incident. Turn on the "paid partnership" label every time.

*This isn't legal advice. Before taking paid sponsors, it's worth a short consult with a Tennessee media attorney.*
